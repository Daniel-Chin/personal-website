# Abstraction boundary in puzzle games
Professor Margret Minsky told me the concept of *abstraction boundary*. We create the digital abstraction over the analog circuits. We create Python over c++. Minsky commented on how abstraction boundaries are broken when students play with breadboard circuits - curly cables picking up signals in the air and so on.  

Remember AntiChamber? 

[spoiler alert]

There is an end-game puzzle where you need to block a laser to open a door. However, the laser is placed at a distance so that if you leave the laser and approach the door, the door is going to close right before you can get through. The solution is to use the laser to give upward momentum to the door - do not open it fully! And then go for the door. Instead of falling from the top of the parabola, the door now first reaches the top of the parabola and then falls down, giving you extra time. 

This puzzle got me thinking for multiple days. Finally beating it was extremely satisfying and left my proud for days. Of course, this puzzle felt "incoherent" with the rest of the game. First of all, the type of puzzle solving skills it tests is drastically different from the puzzle solving skills the game has built up within the player. But it doesn't stop here. 

The game has been training the player to build abstraction. A symbolic puzzle layer. In it, doors are conditional devices. You manage to set it to an open state, and you can go past doors. Of course, the players always know that the doors are simulated using a physics engine; but they soon learn to abstract that away. The final puzzle, however, breaks the abstraction boundary. 

A similar trope was pulled in the Co-op section of Portal 2. Players always knew that the robots had physical collision, but they have been trained to treat it as a to-be-abstracted-away part of the physical layer whose sole purpose is to support the puzzle layer. When the game tells you it's in fact a part of the puzzle layer, it hits hard.

Are there other puzzle games that requires the player to break abstraction boundaries? Both of my examples play with the physical layer. What about other layers in the hierarchy of abstraction?

Escaping the layer you live in reminds me of Zen practices that reach beyond cognition and action. It is impossible. It is necessary --- to beat Antichamber, at least.
